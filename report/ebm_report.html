Expansion Breakaway Model

An Inner Circle Trader setup with one rule added: trade the breakaway fair value gap only when daily volatility is expanding. Built on 2005–2014 data, frozen, then run once on 2015–2020.

Where it stands

The model made money on the Nasdaq-100 holdout and lost it on the S&P 500, EUR/USD, GBP/USD and gold. The Nasdaq profit rests on about three trend days and the t-stat is about 1. Treat it as a lead to forward-test, not a proven edge. The mechanized textbook ICT setups lost money everywhere.

The setup

  1. Regime gateThe 14-day average daily range is at least 1.10 × the 100-day average. Otherwise no trade today.
  2. Liquidity mapAt 09:30: the overnight, previous-day, pre-market, Asia, London and previous cash-session highs above the open, within 1.5 ATR of it.
  3. RunBetween 09:30 and 11:30, price trades through one or more of those levels.
  4. BreakawayA 15-minute candle closes above the highest level that was run, and the breakout leg contains a bullish 15-minute FVG.
  5. EntryLimit buy at the FVG's midpoint (consequent encroachment), live until 12:00. Cancel it if a 15-minute candle closes back below the level.
  6. StopAt the run level. Skip the trade if that's closer than max(0.05 ATR, 2 NQ points) or wider than 0.6 ATR.
  7. ExitAt 15:55 New York, or at the stop. One trade per day. Shorts are the mirror image.

When it trades

The gate was open on 29% of days in 2005–2014 and 37% in 2015–2020, and a setup forms on only some of those, so the model averages about 11 trades a year. Everything else in the day exists to build the liquidity map it reads at the open.

Before you trade it

  • The holdout edge isn't statistically significant. At 58 trades, +0.31R per trade is consistent with luck.
  • It's a trend-day catcher. About two in three trades lose, losing streaks run long, and a few days make each year.
  • It didn't transfer. The same rules lost on the S&P 500 holdout and on FX and gold.
  • The data is Oanda's 1-minute mid price, a proxy for CME futures, and it ends in May 2020. Nothing here covers 2021–2026.
  • At 1% risk per trade, the Nasdaq holdout works out to roughly +3% a year with a 7% drawdown. That's a modest return.

Forward-test it

  1. Add pine/expansion_breakaway_model.pine to a 1, 3 or 5-minute NQ or MNQ chart in TradingView. It's a Pine v6 strategy written to the same rules; it hasn't been compiled in TradingView yet, so small syntax fixes may be needed.
  2. Check the regime panel at the open. When it reads CLOSED, the model has nothing to do today.
  3. Paper-trade 30 to 50 signals before risking money, and size live trades at 0.5–1% risk.
  4. To re-run the research, see docs/RESEARCH.md. scripts/run_ebm.py runs the frozen model on any period.

Data: Oanda one-minute mid prices, January 2005 to May 2020, from the public FutureSharks/financial-data repository. Fills are conservative: limit orders need a one-tick trade-through, and a bar that touches both stop and target counts as a stop. R is profit or loss divided by the planned stop distance, after costs.
